# HomeStars vs. Marido en Alquiler

Pricing, trade-offs and a Dominican Republic launch model

Prepared for Target Innovations • 16 November 2026

### Recommendation

Launch Marido en Alquiler (MEA) as a trusted introductions marketplace: free for homeowners and free professional profiles, then charge a small, clearly disclosed fee for a qualified, mutually accepted connection. Keep your existing quote, chat and completion tools. Introduce optional subscriptions only after proving lead value; defer commission-based job payments until demand and payment operations justify them.

### 1. HomeStars in brief

HomeStars is a Canadian home-services marketplace in the Angi / Instapro family. Homeowners post work, review interested professionals and select whom to contact. Professionals describe their trades and service areas. Its central commercial product is customer introductions, supported by profiles and reviews. [1, 2, 9]

### What its current pricing model actually does

| Item | Verified public position |
| --- | --- |
| Homeowners / profiles | Job posting and professional registration are free. [1, 2] |
| Charge trigger | Professionals pay for a shortlisted lead/contact release, not a guaranteed completed job. The agreement also permits a pro to self-shortlist where that option is offered. [1, 3] |
| Lead price | Varies by likely job size, value, category and location; displayed for the opportunity. No universal public CAD rate card was verified in the pages reviewed. [3] |
| Optional package | Monthly subscription buys credits applied to lead fees. Actual package price, term, renewal and cancellation must be checked in the offered subscription agreement. [3] |
| Risk / refunds | Winning work is not guaranteed. Published lead-fee terms make refunds discretionary and credit-only. Customer work contracts are independent of HomeStars. [3] |

Research boundary: these are current public mechanics, not an account-specific quotation. The registration page links the professional agreement dated 21 January 2025. Historical contractor anecdotes about monthly charges are not treated as current prices.

### The important strategic distinction

Your application is not yet committed to a payment model. Treat “commission marketplace” as an alternative you could build—not as something already working. Copy HomeStars’ useful matching and trust mechanics while retaining MEA’s project records; choose monetization through a local pilot.

## 3. Monetization models: pros and cons

The table compares potential MEA revenue choices. Only the lead mechanism is grounded in HomeStars’ documented offering; proposed Dominican fees and subscription terms are recommendations.

| Criterion | Qualified contact fee — recommended first | Monthly subscription only | Commission on completed jobs |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| When paid | After homeowner consent and pro confirmation of the disclosed introduction price. | Fixed recurring fee for access/tools; optional credits can be a later variant. | A share of job value when a qualifying job/payment completes. |
| Platform pros / cons | + Collect without handling the entire job payment. − Must prove lead quality and manage credits/disputes. | + Predictable recurring revenue. − Difficult to sell before there is steady demand; churn risk. | + Revenue follows successful work. − Hard to verify off-platform cash jobs; more payment/support complexity. |
| Professional pros / cons | + No mandatory monthly bill; control over each purchase. − Pays for an opportunity even when not hired. | + Predictable budget and tools. − Pays in quiet months; must understand any limits/renewal. | + Usually pays only after a win. − Higher fee per success; payout timing and fee disputes can arise. |
| Homeowner pros / cons | + Free entry and a manageable shortlist. − Too much charging can deter good pros or inflate quotes. | + Free comparison if homeowners remain free. − Thin supply if smaller pros cannot justify a plan. | + Potential payment records and structured support. − Checkout friction; protection is not automatic. |
| Best fit for MEA | Pilot first, with a maximum of three consenting introductions per job and clear invalid-lead remedies. | Offer later to proven repeat users; avoid mandatory annual commitments at launch. | Consider later for repeatable booked services through a suitable payment partner, not as launch dependency. |

## 4. Adapt it to the Dominican Republic

### Local evidence and practical adaptations

BCRD’s ENIEF 2023 reports that 55.9% received their principal income in cash. Separately, World Bank Global Findex data show 64.78% (about 65%) of people aged 15+ had a financial/mobile account in 2024. These are different measures and years—not the cash share of contractor transactions. They support testing both cash-compatible job settlement and convenient digital platform-fee collection. [7, 8]

Start in one tightly served Santo Domingo zone, or wherever your trusted contractor network is strongest. Pilot plumbing, electrical repairs and AC maintenance; validate the categories locally. Recruit through ferreterías, building managers and existing referrals. These are launch hypotheses, not measured market rankings.

Use Spanish, neighborhood/sector and landmark-based location fields, mobile-friendly photos and explicit contact consent. Test a WhatsApp handoff after introduction; keep accepted scope, price and completion records in MEA. WhatsApp adoption and willingness to pay still need interviews.

Verify identity with consent, check applicable trade credentials and references, and label exactly what was checked. Keep competence badges separate from paid plans. Define complaints before offering a funded guarantee.

### What must change in your existing app

| Stage | Necessary change |
| --- | --- |
| Before charging | Add verified requests, shortlist/consent states, explicit fee acceptance, qualification criteria and moderation. Gate phone/email everywhere—not only in the UI. Current public contractor data, client-info-by-project access and unrestricted direct chat can bypass an introduction charge. Preserve existing conversations; never charge retroactively. |
| Billing pilot | Use a hosted payment provider for platform fees. Add an idempotent purchase/entitlement ledger, confirmed-payment webhooks, spending caps, receipts and reversals. Grant contact access only after confirmed entitlement. The disabled legacy card-storage path is not a shortcut. |
| Trust operations | Cap paid introductions at three. Accept invalid-number, confirmed duplicate/nonexistent or platform-mismatched-job claims within a proposed 7-day window; target a decision within 2 business days. Losing a valid bid alone is not an invalid lead. State cash refunds versus optional credits clearly. |
| Later expansion | Add subscription credits and repeat-service tools after repeat usage. Reconsider commissions only when reliable booking/payment records and support capacity exist. Obtain local review of commercial/privacy/payment terms before monetization. |

### Economics: test value before increasing fees

Illustrative only: 200 qualified requests × 60% reaching introductions × 2 paid contacts × RD$200 = RD$48,000 gross fees; a 10% reversal allowance leaves RD$43,200 before marketing, staff, processing, tax and verification costs. This is not a revenue forecast.

Contractor check: RD$200 per lead at a 20% win rate costs RD$1,000 per won job. On RD$5,000 labor revenue with 40% contribution before lead cost, half that contribution disappears into leads. Lower the fee or improve conversion before scaling.

## 5. Pilot decision and source notes

### An eight-week validation plan

Proposed scope: 20 vetted professionals, 50 homeowner interviews and one service zone. Weeks 1–2: interviews, free assisted matching and pricing disclosure tests. Weeks 3–4: verify demand, response times and successful matches. Weeks 5–8: test modest paid introductions with spending caps, then compare repeat use and contractor economics.

Track qualified requests, time to first response, shortlist-to-contact conversion, paid-contact-to-hire rate, invalid-lead reversals, repeat paying pros and contribution after acquisition/support costs. Suggested pilot gates—not industry benchmarks: ≥80% reachable requests, ≥70% answered within two operating hours, ≤10% invalid-lead reversals, and positive contractor economics. Pause charging if quality is weak.

Decision recommended now: approve a lead-first pilot, keep homeowners free, defer mandatory subscriptions and full job-payment handling, and prioritize trust/moderation plus contact permissions before billing. Research the model; do not copy HomeStars branding, content or contractual language.
